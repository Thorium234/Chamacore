---

Your first question

> If the funds are in the bank, how does the system know?



It doesn't automatically know just because you have a bank account.

You need some form of bank integration or bank statement reconciliation.

There are several possibilities.

Option 1: Manual recording

The treasurer logs into the bank, sees:

Bank balance:
KSh 450,000

and records/imports the information into your system.

This is the easiest V1.

But the application should distinguish:

System balance
vs
Bank balance

For example:

System ledger       KSh 450,000
Bank statement      KSh 450,000
Difference          KSh 0

That's reconciliation.


---

Option 2: Bank statement import

The bank may provide statements in formats such as CSV/PDF/other supported formats.

The treasurer could upload the statement:

Bank
 ↓
Statement
 ↓
NUCSWG System
 ↓
Transactions
 ↓
Reconciliation

The system can then compare bank transactions against its internal records.

This is much more realistic for an early product than assuming you can connect directly to every Kenyan bank.


---

Option 3: Direct bank integration

Eventually, if the bank provides an appropriate API/integration channel and the Chama is eligible, you can have:

NUCSWG API
      ↓
Bank API
      ↓
Account transactions
      ↓
NUCSWG

Then the system can periodically retrieve transactions.

But don't design V1 around this assumption.

Bank integrations depend on the specific bank, account type, agreements, authentication mechanisms and access they provide.

Now your second question

> Payments are from M-Pesa and enter the bank. How will the system know?



This is where we need to separate M-Pesa payment processing from bank reconciliation.

Imagine:

Member
  │
  │ KSh 5,000
  ↓
M-Pesa
  │
  ↓
NUCSWG bank account

There are potentially two different records:

M-Pesa transaction
        +
Bank transaction

Your system needs to connect them.


---

Example

John is supposed to contribute:

Monthly contribution = KSh 5,000

John pays using M-Pesa.

M-Pesa produces something like:

Transaction reference: ABC123XYZ
Amount: KSh 5,000
Time: ...

Your system receives the relevant payment information through the appropriate M-Pesa integration/callback mechanism.

Then:

ABC123XYZ
       ↓
Member John
       ↓
NUCSWG
       ↓
Contribution
       ↓
May 2026 contribution
       ↓
KSh 5,000

Now the application knows:

> John has paid his May contribution.



Later, the money reaches the Chama's bank account.

The bank might show:

Credit: KSh 5,000
Reference: ...

Your system can reconcile that bank transaction against the payment/financial transaction it already recorded.


---

This reveals something important

We shouldn't make:

Contribution

the same thing as:

Bank transaction

or:

M-Pesa transaction

They are different concepts.

I'd eventually model something closer to:

Member
   │
   ↓
Contribution
   │
   ↓
Financial Transaction
   │
   ├──────── M-Pesa Transaction
   │
   └──────── Bank Transaction

This gives us traceability.


---

Think of it like this

Suppose NUCSWG receives:

John       KSh 5,000
Mary       KSh 5,000
Peter      KSh 10,000

The application records:

Expected/recorded contributions
───────────────────────────────
John       5,000
Mary       5,000
Peter     10,000
───────────────────────────────
Total     20,000

Then the bank says:

Bank credits
────────────────
ABC123     5,000
DEF456     5,000
GHI789    10,000
────────────────
Total     20,000

The reconciliation engine says:

System: KSh 20,000
Bank:   KSh 20,000

✓ Reconciled

But suppose the bank says:

Bank: KSh 15,000
System: KSh 20,000

Now:

⚠ KSh 5,000 difference

The treasurer investigates.

That's the kind of problem our production system should solve.
